<h1> Some Useful Tools</h1>

<h2>[Unit Conversions](https://halas.rice.edu/unit-conversions)</h2>
<br>
Page created by Prof Naomi Halas at Rice University, super useful for spectroscopists to quickly convert frequency and energy units.


<h2>[Graphing Calculator](https://www.desmos.com/calculator)</h2>
<br>
Desmos Graphing Calculator is useful when you want a quick plot of a function or compare different functions.


<h2>[Optics Toolbox](https://toolbox.lightcon.com/)</h2>
<br>
If you are developing optical setups, here is a complete toolbox from Light Conversion to figure out non-linear optics, dispersion, etc.

<h1>Reads that may interest you...</h1>

<h2>[From Intuition to Creation: How AI Changes the Architecture of Intellectual Work](https://pubs.acs.org/jpclcd/article/17/30/8506/5237234/From-Intuition-to-Creation-How-AI-Changes-the)</h2>

A thought-provoking opinion piece by Prof Juan Bisquert about what role LLMs will take in the life of researchers. To quote the author: "When the distance between intuition and creation becomes very small, will we become intellectually passive—or will we be free to spend more of our lives having ideas?"


<h2>[Louis Brus's Advice to Scientists](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41565-023-01564-3#question-33)</h2>

In this [Q&A](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41565-023-01564-3#question-33), Louis Brus, winner of the 2023 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, describes his journey from the first encounter with quantum dots — the "bump" — to winning the Nobel Prize along with Moungi Bawendi and Aleksey Yekimov. He also gives inspiring advice to scientists at the early stage of their careers.


<h2>[Grad School Tips](https://github.com/jbhuang0604/awesome-tips)</h2>

For an academic or any other professional career, it is useful to develop good skills in writing papers and e-mails, talking, presentation, and working with a team. Here is a compilation of [useful resources and tips](https://github.com/jbhuang0604/awesome-tips) (not made by us).


<h2>[Why Bell Labs Worked](https://1517.substack.com/p/why-bell-labs-worked)</h2>

[An article](https://1517.substack.com/p/why-bell-labs-worked) about the place that birthed so many innovations of the 20th century that we are still building on today — laser, transistor, CCD, solar cell, digital signal processing.... It is amazing how all of it came from one institution.
